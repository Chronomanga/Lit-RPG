# Design a System the story can use

This is an original working reference. Its exercises are design tests, not claims about a real game's balance. See [SOURCES.md](SOURCES.md) for the research that informed the workflow.

## Begin with the purpose

Decide what the System makes possible in this particular story. Does it reward exploration, dangerous combat, craftsmanship, care, territory, promises, knowledge, or a narrower activity? What behavior does that reward structure encourage? Who benefits from it? What would a dishonest person try?

Separate the author's actual rules from characters' beliefs. A character may misunderstand a hidden condition; the underlying rule still needs a coherent explanation when it becomes decisive. A mystery about the System's creator does not require uncertainty about a skill's immediate cost.

## Specify the core before adding layers

| Design area | Questions to settle |
| --- | --- |
| Origin and authority | What is known about its arrival? What is author-known but hidden? Who, if anyone, can change it? Can its messages be mistaken or deceptive? |
| Access | Who gets it, when, and through what event? Does everyone see the same interface? Can another person read a private status? |
| Identity | What does a class specialize in? How is it obtained? Can it change? What does rarity mean in this world? |
| Measurement | What does each stat actually measure? What actions change when it rises? Is a value a rank, a physical quantity, or an abstract game unit? |
| Resources | Current and maximum values, expenditure, recovery rate, recovery conditions, storage, and transfer. |
| Advancement | Qualifying achievements, credit sharing, thresholds, when leveling activates, rewards, choices, and whether resources refill. |
| Failure | Injury, death, respawn, lost items, retreat, broken trust, missed opportunities, and recovery. Choose what applies. |
| Information | What does the hero know? What does the reader need now? Which rules are hidden, and what evidence reveals them later? |

Add equipment grades, crafting trees, factions, pets, professions, titles, resistances, or currencies when they create useful choices. A large skill menu does not require giving the hero every skill. Define a layer before using it to settle an important conflict.

## Write an ability as a usable rule

Record: **effect, valid target, range, duration, trigger, prerequisite, cost, limitation, recovery, failure, interactions, and first demonstration**. Not every field needs a number. A passive power might be limited by a narrow target, incompatible build choice, or an exposed weakness rather than a mana fee.

Test three uses:

1. Ordinary: the obvious intended use.
2. Clever: an unusual application that still follows the rule.
3. Impossible or failed: a tempting use the rule does not permit.

If a loophole works reliably, ask why other people have not built their lives around it. A credible answer might be rare access, hidden knowledge, dangerous cost, missing infrastructure, or an established misunderstanding. “Everyone else was stupid” seldom makes the world richer.

## Make a legendary class specific

Rarity can mean scarce access, dangerous prerequisites, lost knowledge, unusual combinations, political restriction, or simply a System rank. Define which meaning applies. The class should change the hero's options noticeably.

Then distinguish its advantage from mastery. A novice can gain an extraordinary tool while lacking aim, stamina, knowledge, timing, or judgment. Some stories intentionally grant broad dominance; choose challenges that still matter, such as protecting others, discovering truth, resolving conflicting duties, or reaching an unavailable place. Do not repeatedly invent a stronger enemy only to cancel every new gain.

## Keep progression arithmetic unambiguous

Choose either cumulative lifetime thresholds or an experience bar that resets. Label the method. For a resetting bar, 100 XP earned against a 100-XP threshold reaches the next level and leaves zero XP unless overflow is specified. Do not count the same reward again on the next bar.

Define the level-up protocol. It might increase maximum energy, restore current energy, grant allocation points, or require sleep to activate. None of those follows automatically from “level up.” Record the rule before a clutch victory depends on it.

Use resource accounting:

**Ending current value = starting current value − valid costs + earned recovery or gains**, capped at the applicable maximum. Apply events in actual time order when a maximum changes or a cap could discard recovery.

Example: 40 Focus, three uses at 12 each, no recovery during combat. The result is 4 Focus. Recovery outside combat is 2 Focus per minute, so reaching the 12 needed for another use takes four uninterrupted eligible minutes. If a level increases the maximum to 45 without refilling, current Focus remains 4. See [EXAMPLES.md](EXAMPLES.md).

Track cooldowns separately from resources. Having enough mana does not finish a cooldown. Establish whether interruptions reset recovery, and whether equipment modifies the cost or the maximum. Use only calculations that matter to the story.

## Choose reward logic and power scale

For each gain, record its cause, eligibility, recipient, amount, timing, repeatability, and what it enables. Combat need not be the only route; choose activities consistent with the world. Trust, access, knowledge, equipment, and rescue can be rewards without XP.

Test a repeatable reward against low-risk farming. Either allow the consequence and build a world that uses it, or establish a condition that prevents it. Do not patch a loophole only after the hero has exploited it conveniently.

Plan comparisons rather than assuming level alone determines victory. Training, terrain, equipment, teamwork, information, counters, and willingness to pay a cost can matter. If level is an absolute barrier in this world, honor it. If a low-level hero can defeat a stronger opponent, show the rule-consistent cause.

## Explain the rule in a scene

The reader needs enough information to understand the choice and recognize the consequence. A compact System message can give the key limit; an attempt can show its effect; a failure can reveal what remains unknown. Follow a newly earned skill with a useful test when the scene allows it.

Keep the private reference more detailed than the prose. When an author-known hidden rule changes, trace its effects through earlier clues, rewards, and future plans. Use [templates/SYSTEM.md](templates/SYSTEM.md) and [templates/STATE.md](templates/STATE.md) as the record.
