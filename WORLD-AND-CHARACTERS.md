# People living inside a System

## A protagonist has reasons, not just a build

Define the immediate goal and why failure matters personally. Add a longer desire, a value under pressure, useful competence, a limitation, and something the character does not understand. A flaw can shape the arc, but not every setback must be punishment for a flaw.

Test agency with a simple question: if the hero chose differently here, would the next event change? An imposed disaster can begin the story; meaningful pursuit, resistance, alliance, investigation, or adaptation should follow. Agency does not mean winning alone or rejecting help.

Keep competence specific. A nurse might recognize shock but not local magic. A gamer might test a cooldown but misunderstand a society that treats levels as religious authority. A mechanic might see an unusual application without knowing who legally owns the machine. Let expertise help while preserving uncertainty and capable local people.

## Introduce significant people naturally

Provide a name or usable identity, a role or relationship, a present aim, and a distinguishing action or voice cue. These can emerge over a few exchanges rather than an introductory paragraph.

Example: “I'm Sella. I run the ferry, and those people are still on my boat.” Her attempt to pull the rope while the hero seeks shelter shows what matters to her. Her role has an immediate use; a later history can wait until it changes a choice.

A mysterious person can withhold their name or allegiance. Still make their visible behavior and effect on the situation clear. Deliberate uncertainty about identity is different from being unable to picture who is speaking.

For a recurring companion, record a goal, competence, vulnerability, reason to cooperate, reason to refuse, and a life outside the hero. The System should not turn everyone into an automatic admirer. Loyalty grows through choices and consequences.

## Make opposition and betrayal causal

An antagonist wants an outcome and has a workable method, resources, a worldview, and limits. Let them act between meetings. A social institution, rival, monster ecology, or the environment can oppose the hero without a secretly evil individual behind every event.

For betrayal, establish access, motive, pressure, opportunity, and a reason the victim trusted the person. Plant evidence that supports the reveal without announcing it. Afterward, change plans, knowledge, safety, or relationships. Do not add a twist solely because the last chapter needs a shock.

## Trace powers into society

Begin with a few consequential rules and ask who responds to them.

| World area | Causal questions |
| --- | --- |
| History | What changed the world? What survived? Who records or disputes that history? |
| Everyday work | How do powers alter farming, transport, construction, communication, care, and risk? What still requires ordinary labor? |
| Food and scarcity | Which resources can be renewed? What cannot be conjured, healed, stored, or transported cheaply? |
| Authority | Who controls training, class access, safe land, artifacts, or knowledge? What constrains them? |
| Economy | What has value because of a power's limits? What does a reliable exploit make cheap or abundant? |
| Communities | What do people celebrate, fear, teach children, argue about, or consider a fair exchange? |
| Danger | What do creatures need? How do they move, spread, recover, or adapt? Why is this settlement vulnerable? |
| Inequality | Who lacks access? How do ordinary people negotiate power differences? Who benefits from a false belief? |

Write a lived consequence before writing a lecture. If healing cannot restore blood, a healer may still depend on donors and transport. That limitation affects rescue, work, law, and trust. Only the part relevant to the scene needs explanation now.

## Plan revelations in layers

1. **Needed immediately:** what the hero faces and the rules required for the next choice.
2. **Useful soon:** a relationship, institution, practice, or historical fact that changes the current goal.
3. **Deliberately hidden:** a question the author understands, with clues and a planned revelation.
4. **Unresolved design:** something not decided yet; do not write it as settled fact.

The author may keep the full origin behind the scenes while showing its visible consequences. If the owner requests a clear explanation of how the world came to be, plan a real answer rather than indefinitely calling every missing explanation a mystery.

## Keep a living world selective

Choose details through attention and stakes. A street celebration can show a local value, a trader's refusal can reveal class law, and an exhausted healer can reveal a limit. Routine locks, inventories, and repairs get space only when they affect the current event. The world feels alive when its people have purposes, not when every object has a paragraph.
