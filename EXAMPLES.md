# Original worked examples

All examples here were created for this playbook. They are not copied from the sources, a complete manuscript, or automatic canon for Kip Quillman or any other project. Use their design principles without forcing their names or rules into a new story.

## 1. A useful brief

**Working premise:** Nara Venn, a bridge inspector, arrives in a city built across moving islands. She wants to reach a stranded ferry carrying her brother before the islands separate. A legendary class lets her move loose objects, but the city treats that class as stolen property. The book promises inventive rescues, power growth, uneasy alliances, and the discovery of who controls the crossings.

This supplies a person, personal goal, special capability, opposition, world question, and experience. It leaves room to invent the route without beginning with centuries of history.

## 2. A class that grants a specific advantage

**Class:** Gravemark, legendary. **Ability:** Draw. Move one visible, loose object of at most ten kilograms toward the user along a clear path, no farther than eight metres. The object travels at walking speed and stops at an obstruction. It cannot target living bodies, held objects, or attached structures. Cost: 12 Focus per use. No cooldown. Starting Focus: 40/40. Recovery: 2 Focus per uninterrupted minute outside combat. A larger maximum does not refill current Focus.

**Ordinary use:** draw a fallen shield across open ground.

**Clever use:** draw a loose, bright lantern from the far end of an unlit passage to reveal movement without walking there first.

**Failed use:** try to pull a sword someone is gripping. The target is invalid; define in the project whether an invalid attempt costs Focus. For this example, it does not.

The rare tool offers reach and control. It does not grant combat training, move a person to safety, rip off a gate, ignore walls, or heal an injury. New applications need to follow the established effect.

## 3. Resource and level-up continuity

| Event | Current Focus | Maximum | Explanation |
| --- | ---: | ---: | --- |
| Start | 40 | 40 | Opening state |
| First valid use | 28 | 40 | Spend 12 |
| Second valid use | 16 | 40 | Spend 12 |
| Third valid use | 4 | 40 | Spend 12 |
| Level increases maximum by 5 | 4 | 45 | No refill under this rule |
| Four eligible minutes recovering | 12 | 45 | Gain 2 per minute, 8 total |
| Fourth valid use | 0 | 45 | Spend 12 |

If the prose needs an immediate fourth use before that recovery, the plan must change. Nara can use another approach, ask for help, accept failure, or choose a previously established resource. Do not silently grant free Focus. If the owner approves a different level-up rule, revise the System and every affected scene together.

## 4. Simple explanation with a natural rhythm

**Overloaded:** The north gate used a different fastening system because its previous keeper had replaced the older bolts after a shortage, and the angle of the upper plate meant Nara would need to examine the hinge before deciding whether the mechanism could resist pressure.

**Unnecessarily chopped:** The gate shook. Nara looked. It was weak. Someone screamed. She had no time. She had a power. She needed to use it.

**Clearer flow:** The gate bent under the creature's next blow, and Nara knew it would not hold long enough for the children to cross. She reached for the fallen shield with her new power, drawing it over the stones while Sella hurried the last child toward the ferry.

The third version connects danger, judgment, power, and another person's action. It explains the gate only as much as the choice requires. A longer sentence carries a clear sequence instead of making the scene sound like a list.

## 5. Introduce someone through their goal

“I'm Sella. I run the ferry, and those people are still on my boat.” She pushed the loose mooring rope into Nara's hands. “Help me reach them, then you can ask what happened to the sky.”

The reader learns a name, role, urgent aim, and voice. The world has another person with obligations. Sella's full childhood can wait. If she later refuses to abandon a passenger, the choice already has a basis.

## 6. Make an increase useful

Before: Nara must stand within eight metres to draw the lantern, which puts her within the creature's reach.

Earned improvement: after solving a difficult rescue and choosing the reach branch instead of reduced cost, she can use Draw from ten metres. Weight, speed, and valid targets remain unchanged.

Demonstration: in the next passage, she retrieves the lantern from outside the creature's reach. The earlier danger is visibly easier, but she still cannot pull Sella out of a collapsing boat because living targets remain invalid.

The gain matters, the choice has an opportunity cost, and the restriction continues to shape action. Record the old and new range with the exact chapter where it changes.

## 7. Turn a power limit into a living world

Suppose healing closes wounds but cannot replace lost blood. A rescue service still needs trained caregivers, donors, clean storage, and fast transport. Wealthy districts may have better reserves, and a ferry operator may risk a crossing to reach the poorer shore.

In a scene, show the healer stopping the bleeding and asking for a donor. Explain the full institution later if the hero must choose whom to help or discovers someone controlling access. The limitation creates people and conflict rather than only a line in a magic chart.

This is a separate illustrative world option, not an added Gravemark rule or an assertion that every story must use this healing limit.

## 8. Plan progression beside plot

| Story turn | Hero's choice | Progress | Continuing pressure |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Stranded at the crossing | Help Sella instead of leaving alone | Learns Draw; gains an ally | Brother still missing; Focus limited |
| Find a safe route | Trade information for access | Learns how islands move | Route controlled by an opposing faction |
| Difficult rescue | Choose greater reach over lower cost | Range becomes ten metres | Still cannot target living people |
| Ally conceals a fact | Investigate without abandoning the rescue | Better knowledge of the city | Trust becomes uncertain |
| Final crossing | Use earlier knowledge and cooperate | Skill and relationship payoff | Local rescue resolves; deeper origin remains |

This is a partial planning demonstration, not a required plot or a finished novel. A future chapter must establish each event before a ledger treats it as fact.

## 9. A brief chapter card

**Goal:** reach Sella's stranded ferry. **Pressure:** a creature is damaging the gate while the islands drift apart. **Choice:** spend scarce Focus helping the passengers, accepting that Nara cannot use it to protect herself afterward. **Rule disclosed:** Draw moves loose objects, not people. **Result:** the passengers reach the safe side through a cooperative plan. **Change:** Nara earns Sella's trust and learns where her brother was last seen. **Ending:** the named destination is controlled by the people hunting Gravemark users.

The card gives the chapter a result and a new direction. It does not require a level-up or a surprise betrayal to matter.

## 10. An earned continuation hook

The ferry reached the landing, and Sella finally let go of the rope. Every passenger had crossed.

“Your brother was alive when I saw him,” she said, pointing toward the tower beyond the market. “But if you're going there, cover that mark on your hand.”

This ends after a real rescue while opening a specific question. Another chapter might end quietly after a decision; vary the pattern.

## 11. Separate an audio display from its spoken meaning

Visual reference: `Focus 4/45; recovery +2/min outside combat; Draw cost 12`.

Possible narration: “She had four Focus left. Another pull would cost twelve, and she would need four quiet minutes to recover the difference.”

The spoken line gives the numbers needed for the next decision. It is a writing example, not evidence that generated narration was listened to or approved.

## 12. A useful revision finding

**Finding:** the chapter uses Draw on Sella, but the approved skill excludes living targets.

**Ordinary fix:** change the rescue method to use an eligible object or cooperation, then check positions and Focus again.

**Major alternative:** propose expanding the skill to living targets. That changes the class's power, world consequences, and later challenges; identify it as a canon proposal rather than quietly editing the rule.

The point of an audit is a coherent final scene, not a long error list or an unexplained passing score.
