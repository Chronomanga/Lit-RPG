# Prose and explanation style

These are the owner's writing preferences. Apply them with judgment rather than a sentence-length quota.

1. **Use simpler language.** Common words, clear speakers, and clear action. Danger and emotion remain serious. Explain unfamiliar terms at the point of need.
2. **Keep a natural flow.** Mix sentence lengths to fit thought, movement, emotion, and breath. Do not chop every thought into a separate sentence. A fast scene can still be clear; a quiet scene can still move the story.
3. **Focus on the story's chosen interests.** Action, powers, System growth, betrayal, and discoveries about the world can drive an adventure. Betrayal needs motive and setup; it is not a mandatory surprise in every arc.
4. **Spend explanation where it matters.** Explain a power, choice, threat, relationship, important clue, or meaningful world fact. Compress routine locks, repairs, tools, paperwork, and stock counts unless they change events.
5. **Explain powers through use.** Show what a person tries, what happens, what limits the result, and what it costs. Give rest and practice a useful role. Let later attempts demonstrate improvement.
6. **Reach meaningful payoffs.** A chapter should perform a clear job: a result, a changed relationship, a discovery, a costly failure, or a new decision. Combine setup and test when splitting them only delays the useful event. Do not replace dramatic development with constant rushing.
7. **Cut repetition.** Avoid repeated recaps and restating feelings or facts already clear from the scene. Brief reminders can help after a real gap or when a rule becomes decisive.
8. **Track continuity privately.** Record injuries, resources, knowledge, possessions, locations, and unfinished problems. Put those details into the story when they affect events.
9. **Introduce people when they matter.** Give a new important person a name, role or relationship, present aim, and one distinctive action or voice cue. An intentional mystery can hide identity; the scene must still give the reader something concrete to follow.
10. **Make the world feel inhabited.** Let people want things, disagree, work, celebrate, fear, adapt, and make choices beyond the hero's immediate needs. A few consequential details can do more than a history lecture.

## Explanation test

Ask of a passage: what can the reader understand, picture, anticipate, or feel after this explanation that they could not before? If the answer is nothing relevant, cut or compress it. If a later victory depends on the rule, establish it before the victory or make the discovery itself a credible part of the struggle.

Clarity does not require answering an intentional mystery early. A hidden System origin is fine when the reader understands what the hero can presently do and why their next decision matters.
