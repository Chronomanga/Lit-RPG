## Original source capsule

Lecture 1 covers practice, personal process, sustainable scheduling, and feedback, rather than detailed plot, character, or worldbuilding techniques. Sanderson stresses repeated writing and finishing; a course supplies tools to test, not a publication guarantee. Outlining and discovery occupy a flexible spectrum, and a writer's useful mixture may change. Consistency means a workable recurring pattern, not necessarily daily quotas. Protect writing time and personal relationships. Workshops should serve the author's intended story: readers report reactions and successes; the author listens, records, waits, and revises deliberately instead of defending or accepting every proposed fix. He mentions adding a sense of progress to pacing, but does not teach the formal promises/progress/payoff framework here. Plot, setting, character, and business are announced as course subjects; detailed prescriptions for those areas must come from other sources. His productivity rates and career anecdotes are illustrations, not universal schedules or measured success probabilities. Apply this by choosing a sustainable cadence, testing process tools, completing drafts, and separating reader reactions from proposed remedies.
