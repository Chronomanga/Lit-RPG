### Parameters, limits, and honest choices

An ability entry should be usable while outlining a scene. For example, an original skill might pull one loose metal object from within six metres, cost 12 energy, and require a clear view. It cannot move an object another person is gripping. The interesting choice is then concrete: which object helps, whether line of sight can be gained, and what using the energy leaves the hero unable to do. That example is new; it is not a source example or proposed story canon.

For a passive ability, a limit may be narrow applicability, vulnerability elsewhere, a prerequisite, or an unavailable competing choice. Explain the constraint that actually matters instead of adding an arbitrary fee to every harmless effect. The source strongly favors costs; the playbook should turn that into understandable tradeoffs rather than a universal mathematical rule.

Give opponents their own workable options. A constraint that never influences a choice is merely decorative. A clever workaround may succeed once, but record why it works and what prevents unlimited repetition. If the story permits reliable repetition, account for how the world would already use it.

### Reward logic and pacing

Before writing the first gain, choose what the system rewards and what kind of behavior that encourages. Combat, construction, healing, exploration, negotiation, and discovery can support different kinds of stories; do not grant all of them XP automatically just to fill a list. A noncombat success can also pay off through access, knowledge, trust, equipment, or a solved problem rather than a numerical reward.

For any repeatable XP event, decide what qualifies as completion, whether low-risk repetition still counts, who receives credit, and whether repeated attempts change the reward. These are project worksheet extensions, not details claimed to appear in the unseen source tracker. Test a proposed rule against a harmless repeatable action before relying on it in a major scene. If it creates an unlimited loop, either accept that consequence in the world or revise the rule before it becomes canon.

A reward should change a future possibility, settle an immediate need, or force a new decision. Record the achieved result and the remaining problem together. Frequent early gains can provide momentum while later progress comes through better control, new applications, deeper knowledge, relationships, or a difficult specialization. Neither every scene nor every chapter needs a notification.

### Tracking and verification

Keep a short rule reference plus a chapter record. Update the record after the chapter's events settle. Check resource arithmetic, cooldowns against elapsed time, advancement requirements, and inventory changes before using them to resolve the next obstacle. Do not invent numbers first and assume a later extraction tool can make them agree.

If AI assists with extracting prior facts, require the source location, preserve uncertainty, and compare the result with the draft before accepting it into the record. An omitted or contradictory entry needs review; plausible output is not confirmation. The video describes the speaker performing his own check, which supports verification rather than blind trust.
